01 / Numbers

Every number, from sixteen rows and two moves

The memory stores sixteen symbols — the digits 0–9 and the places 10, 100, … 10⁶ — and two relations, + and ×. No number has a row. 347 is not stored; it is reached.

3×100+4×10+7→a place for 347

Each term is one hop of × on a digit and a place anchor; each + hop chains the next term onto the state. The output of one step is the input of the next. The result is a point in the same space as the sixteen rows, computed, never written.

Reading it back is one light per step

Walking the chain backwards — one +⁻¹ hop, then one light over the sixteen rows — shows the last term and nothing else. Hop again, the next term. On numbers never seen in training, seven places from units to millions, the walk reads every term exactly.

347, step 17 +9.5 · everything else ≈ −10.5step 24, 10step 33, 100

What did not work, and why it mattered

Our first version made the anchors ordinary nodes and let the model superpose them. It read 347 and 437 as the same thing (cosine 0.993): a hop is linear, so a hop on a superposition is a superposition of hops, and the number collapses into the bag {3, 100, 4, 10, 7} with no record of what is paired with what. The fix was not a bigger model. The graph is directed, and direction carries the pairing. Trained on the directed walk, the same sixteen rows read back exactly.

Learning = as a relation, in contrast, fails at everything — it cannot even memorise single facts. Evaluation is a light over the memory, not a move in it. This one negative shaped everything below.

The point
A place can exist in the memory without a row for it. That is the same claim part one made about missing links, now made for objects the data never contained at all. The memory learned no arithmetic: + and × are moves that keep terms separable. Adding, comparing and deciding are done over the memory, by a program that reads lights and chooses moves.

02 / Programs

Operations found from three worked examples

Reasoning is not another relation in the space. It is a small machine over the memory: six anonymous slots that hold places, a dozen primitives, and a program that reads lights and chooses moves. The programs are not written by us. They are found.

The machine

Three kinds of primitive, none of which knows what a carry or a verdict is. Moves: walk a slot's place one step back, bind or unbind two values, shift a digit, call another program. Lights: read a slot against the 0–19 line, the −10–9 line, the times table, or the digit order. Writes: set, copy, write a digit to the result, answer. The controller keeps no state of its own: everything it needs is in a slot. With the verdict kept in a hidden state, comparison generalised to 0.24 at nine digits; with the verdict in a slot, 1.000.

The search

A program is an initialisation, a loop body, and the slot whose emptiness ends the loop. We show the machine three worked examples of an operation — the steps of 12+11, 383+47, 19+65 as flat lists, no loop marked — and search for a program whose loop reproduces them. Acceptance requires exactness on held-out cases two digits longer than any shown; without that rule, a first multiplication carried a spare SHIFT, perfect to four digits, 0.5 at nine.

The addition program, as found

# found from the traces of 12+11, 383+47, 19+65 — slots 0,1: the two numbers as places
init   SET 2<0                # slot 2 holds the carry: none yet
body   LOAD 0>3   LOAD 1>4    # walk each number one place back: two digits
       BIND 3,4   BIND 3,2     # digit ⊙ digit ⊙ carry, in the value code
       READ 3                  # one light over the 0..19 line
       SETD 5   SETC 2         # the digit into slot 5, its carry back into slot 2
       WRITE 5                 # next digit of the result
stop   slot 4 empty            # chosen by the search: nothing left to walk

Subtraction, found next with addition callable, is the same cycle with the memory's inverse move (UNBIND) and the −10..9 line (READN). In the earlier machine, found from the addition program alone, it came out as an idiom of its own — the borrow added to y's digit and subtracted once, a permanent zero kept in a slot for the stop rule, two dead instructions — exact at every length.

The claim
Operations are induced from three worked examples and generalise in length without limit in the range tested. The controller must be stateless and the context must live in memory; this is what makes a program found on two-digit examples exact on nine.

03 / One memory

The programs live in the same table as the numbers

A found program is not kept in a file. Each instruction is bound to four role relations — opcode and three arguments — into one place; four to six instructions are chained into a page; a program is a few pages. The number table of section 01 was continued with 36 instruction-symbol rows and 4 role relations: one table, one +, one ×.

In plain words: adding a skill to this memory is a write. Append its pages, and calls to it are readable through the role operators that already exist. Numbers, facts and procedures are the same kind of object in the same space — and, as in part one, a computation over them is a walk whose cost does not grow with the size of the table.

04 / Reasoning over stories

Transitive inference from three worked stories

The same machine, with a story memory: a fact is subject⊗SUBJECT + relation⊗RELATION + object⊗OBJECT, three role relations trained once on random triples in 25 seconds. A story is a set of such places in working memory. The names are fresh random rows the memory has never seen.

Two reads were added: the fact keyed by a name and a relation, verified by reading its subject and relation back — a light, never a threshold — and a whole-fact check. From three worked stories of at most three links each, the search found the chain-following program, the YES / NO / UNKNOWN decision that calls it, and the program that refuses to propagate a non-transitive relation (knows), each at generation 1.

Two lessons we keep: every read the machine makes is verified by a light, never by a threshold (a keyed read separated present from absent facts by 0.72 vs 0.71 cosine — useless as a cutoff, exact as a read-back); and the first "3–7% errors" were our generator's labels, not the machine. Then we went to an external benchmark.

05 / CLUTRR

Kinship chains of ten hops from stories of two and three

CLUTRR (Sinha et al., 2019) asks: given a chain of family relations between people — Ann's mother's brother's daughter — what is Ann to the last person? Training stories have 2–3 hops, test stories 2–10. We use the graph form of the benchmark, the setting of the strongest published systems.

First attempt · the negativeWe wrote the algebra, and it got worse every time we touched it

The obvious route is a family algebra — up, down, side, spouse, reduce by rules — with the twenty words learned as paths in it. The words came out right (father = up♂, niece = side-down♀, all twenty). Every hand-written or hand-repaired rule made the generalisation worse: 54/62 training compositions consistent, then over-generalisation, then 25/62. Three hours; recorded, abandoned.

Second attempt · the resultEach relation word is a learned operator. Nothing else.

Twenty words, twenty block-diagonal operators of the ResonatE kind, on 64-dimensional places (four blocks of 16×16 per word; about twenty thousand complex numbers in all). A training story (r₁, r₂ → r₃) says only: applying the chain's operators to any random place must land where the target word's operator lands. No steps, no genders, no rules, no meanings. A test chain is the product of its operators on a probe, named by the closest word's single hop, averaged over 64 probes; it abstains when the margin is below the 95th percentile of wrong-answer margins on validation.

The first run of this code, at 4,000 steps and 8 probes, gave 0.78 at ten hops; the whole gap was convergence and read noise, not capacity or a missing primitive.

* The abstentions
All fourteen abstentions at length 3 are one chain, wife → son → grandmother, which is either mother or mother-in-law — and the test set labels it both ways, eight to six. The length-9 abstentions are walks that can return to oneself (father → wife → son is me or my brother, so a later daughter → sister is a daughter or a niece); the two candidate words light within 0.01–0.04 of each other. The benchmark's labels assume the walk never returns to self; the operators see that it can. Where a model that must answer would score these by luck, this one says "two answers".

Controls

With the training targets randomly permuted, the same run gives exact 0.00 and abstain 1.00 at every length: the operators learn nothing consistent and the margin test refuses everything. The model sees only the sequence of relation words — no names, genders or text. Length-2 test chains are the training table (38 of 38 present, as they must be); chains of three and more never occur in training.

What it is not

It is not reading English: the input is the benchmark's relation graph, as for CTP and the GNN baselines; the text setting (BERT, LSTM readers at 0.2–0.4) is a different task. It is one seed. And a kinship algebra is small — twenty operators — which is exactly why the products compose without drift over ten hops; larger vocabularies are untested.

The lesson
Do not write the algebra. The version with no rules, trained only on "this chain lands where that word lands", is the one that generalises — the same emergence part one measured on biokg's relation families, now doing inference. And abstention is a first-class outcome: exact where it knows, silent where it does not, and, on this benchmark, silent precisely where the benchmark is ambiguous.

06 / What we learned

Five principles, each bought with a negative

  • Evaluation is not a move
    = as a relation cannot memorise single facts; learned operator generators memorise and do not generalise (fourteen configurations at 0.000); a successor walk is not an adder in three parametrisations. Equality is a light over the memory.
  • Superposition keeps what, direction keeps pairing, multiplication keeps value
    The bag encoding reads 347 as 437. The directed chain reads every term back. The phase code adds exactly, carries free, and has no order — which is why addition is done one place at a time, never as one value.
  • The controller is stateless
    Verdict in the hidden state: 0.24 at nine digits. Verdict in a slot: 1.000. Every decision must be local to the current lights; then a program found on two-digit examples runs on nine.
  • Nobody discovers addition
    Blank-slate search has no slope. Three worked examples give it one; a neighbouring program gives it another. Learning from examples is what it looks like here.
  • Do not write the algebra
    Hand-written kinship rules got worse with every repair. Twenty learned operators and one objective reached 0.99. Depth lives in the operator, not in rows: a memory grows by continuation, with its moves still learning.

07 / What we can and cannot conclude

Observed, and not established

Observed

  • Unseen numbers below 10⁹ are composed, decomposed, compared, added and multiplied exactly from 18 stored rows, on one seed, on thousands of never-trained cases per length.
  • Five arithmetic programs and three logic programs were induced from three worked examples each and are exact on lengths never shown; the search never saw the ground-truth programs.
  • Programs stored as pages in the number table execute from it; a new program's name row needs no training.
  • Ten-hop CLUTRR at 0.99 exact and ≤ 1% wrong on the full test set, with every remaining abstention traceable to an ambiguity in the benchmark.

Not established

  • Seeds. Every table here is one seed. Between three widths the CLUTRR numbers moved by one or two stories per length; the arithmetic tables have not been re-seeded at all.
  • Baselines we owe: an NPI-style program-induction baseline and a VSA / holographic baseline on the same tasks. Program induction from traces is known (NPI, DreamCoder); binding and superposition are known (HRR, VSA). What is new is a learned relational geometry doing both, exactly, in one table — and that comparison has not been run.
  • The machine's primitives — about a dozen moves, lights and writes — are ours. The programs are found; the instruction set is not.
  • The proposer is random mutation seeded by examples. It found each operation in seconds to minutes, but it is scaffolding for measuring the landscape, not a learner.
  • CLUTRR here is graph input; reading the English is a separate, unbuilt step. Multiplication at nine digits is limited by the memory's range (0.893), and the range is grown by continuation, not unbounded.
  • No real-graph result yet: compositional queries on wikikg2 or biokg from three worked examples is the experiment that would connect this page to part one's tables.
